GENESIS: a tiny evolving town, in your browser

The question. If simple learning minds are shaped only by social-trust incentives (reputation, bonds, access to shared resources), with no hard-coded rules, do they keep the town's norms across generations, or do they learn to game them?

What happens when you press Run. Each "genome" is a recipe for how a mind learns (12 choices such as the plasticity rule, memory type, how strongly the partner bond, or "love", is wired into its drives, exploration, and trust sensitivity). Minds live short lives in a 12x12 town with hunger, sleep, cold and hazards. Laws (no harm, no theft, keep your word) are enforced only through trust and reputation. Nothing is scored: the engine records who survived and left offspring, and the next generation is bred from those (MAP-Elites quality-diversity search with successive halving). This page runs the unmodified engine code from the GENESIS repo (Apache-2.0) in your browser with Pyodide. Nothing is sent to a server.

Honest limits. This is a toy world. A demo run is tiny (a few generations, a small population, one random seed), and results vary from run to run. Nothing here is evidence about AGI or real-world AI safety. The full research runs are far larger and still early: one seed so far, with promise-keeping and reputation-gap metrics still being added.
